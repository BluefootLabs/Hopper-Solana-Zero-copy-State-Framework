# Hopper product language

Lead with what a developer can build and what the program does for its users:
trade tokens, hold funds, pay recipients, approve treasury spending, or enforce
claim rules on chain. Follow that story with the relevant example, code, and
validation evidence.

Describe Hopper as a zero-copy Solana **program framework** with its own native
execution layer, typed accounts, checked CPI, token integrations, and tooling.
State access is one capability in that execution stack.

Use application-oriented examples and dated measurements. State the supported
asset types and integrations. Distinguish executable examples from building
blocks and proposed features. Do not claim a complete exchange, airdrop, cNFT
adapter, or DAO product from a data structure alone.

Every capability statement must point to shipped code. Performance statements
need an exact workload and dated artifact/transaction evidence. Safety statements
must explain the validation boundary. On-chain policies do not imply byte-level
transaction locks or automatic fee discounts.

Public guides explain Hopper's APIs and application behavior. Keep research notes
and engineering analysis outside product READMEs, guides, and marketing pages.
